## En iyi 5 aday

1. **Doppelglow** — 79/100: Exact string returned zero hits on web, Play, App Store, Steam, YouTube, Instagram, TikTok and in US trademark aggregators. Near items are only the doppelganger-themed Steam 'The Doppel' and the non-game DOPPEL marks of Doppel Inc. 'Doppel' (double) plus 'glow' covers both the shadow-double mechanic and the neon look, and it suits the 2-shadow stage. Downsides: Turkish players may find it hard to spell or say ('dopelglov'). Conflict safety is capped below 10 because WIPO, EUIPO, TURKPATENT, USPTO and the direct store APIs were not queried.
2. **Ghostrail** — 68/100: No game, app or mark named Ghostrail was found. Existing uses are a 2014 security paper, the ghostrail.org crypto privacy protocol (an unregistered software use that could be filed later) and a fiction story. 'Rail' points to ghost trains, which do not match the mechanic, and the name can be misread as 'Ghost Trail', which collides with the iOS game Ghost Trail and an itch.io ghost-path game. Minor 'rail' slang risk. Official databases were not checked.
3. **Umbrix** — 65/100: No Play, App Store or Steam title found (web-search level only). Other uses of the exact name: a boss in Roblox Tower Defense X, a Discord logging bot (an app with the exact name), umbrix.dev (cyber threat intelligence), an open-source VPN still in design, a beer, and an Indian Class 18 application. The 'Umbra' namespace is crowded on Steam and the App Store. Short and easy to say in all six languages.
4. **Ombrix** — 64/100: Punchy, 6 letters, with a clear 'ombra/ombre' shadow root and a playful Asterix-style '-ix' ending. It does not express the delayed-replay idea. No source at all could be reached (all hosts blocked, search budget used up), and the research flags the '-ix' pattern as prone to earlier registrations. It is also one letter away from Umbrix, which has several software uses. Its conflict status is entirely unverified.
5. **Echoveer** — 62/100: No exact match on Play, App Store, Steam or in the general web. The 'Echo' prefix is crowded (EchoVerse, Echovo, Echoveil, Echovore, many 'Echo ...' games). The social and trademark searches did not run. 'Veer' is unknown to most non-English players, pronunciation varies (German 'Echo-feer', Turkish 'ç'), and misspellings such as Echover or Echo Veer would hurt search.

## Gerekçeler (aday üreticilerin notları)

- **Echotrail**: 9 chars, one coined word that is easy to say (EK-oh-trail). It names the core mechanic directly: a delayed copy of your path that chases you. Built from neutral words and has no negative reading in EN/TR/DE/IT/FR/ES. Possible store title: 'Echotrail: Neon Shadow Run' (26 chars, under the 30 limit).
- **Shadowloop**: 10 chars. It keeps the literal 'shadow' from the Turkish title, and 'loop' makes it clearly different from Shadow Fight-style names. Both halves are understood worldwide (loop is common in TR/DE/FR/IT/ES tech slang) and neither has a bad meaning. Fits the replay idea and the endless mode.
- **Glowtrace**: 9 chars. It fits the neon glow look (purple/teal/gold on navy) and the path-tracing mechanic. 'Trace' is the same word in French and is easy to read in the other target languages. Short, safe and works as a brand.
- **Lumbra**: 6 chars, very brandable, says the same in most languages (LOOM-bra). It sums up the game: a light character hunted by its own shadow. It also quietly echoes the 'Lumos' in the developer brand without using the Harry Potter spell itself. Spanish 'lumbre' means fire/glow, which is positive. Only minor caveat: a faint resemblance to the medical word 'lumbar', with no negative meaning in any target language.
- **Retrace**: 7 chars, a real English word that is also a French verb, so it is intuitive. It describes the mechanic exactly with no violent or dark tone. Spanish 'retraso' (delay) sounds a bit like it, which happens to fit the delay mechanic and is neutral. Pair it with a descriptor for search, e.g. 'Retrace: Escape Your Shadow' (27 chars).
- **Outpace**: 7 chars, short and positive, and it signals reflex and speed. It avoids the famous 'OutRun' name. Italian 'pace' means peace, so there is a pleasant accidental reading and no negative meaning anywhere. Because it is generic, use it with a subtitle: 'Outpace: Dodge Your Shadow' (26 chars).
- **Echo Escape**: 11 chars. The light alliteration makes it memorable, and it states the goal (don't get caught) more plainly than any other candidate. It avoids the 'Dark Echo' pattern because it has no dark/horror wording. Simple words that players in all six languages understand.
- **Ghostrail**: 9 chars. The 'ghost' idea is familiar from racing-game ghost laps that replay your previous run, which is exactly this mechanic, so arcade players get it fast. It is playful rather than scary and is fine in all target languages.
- **Neon Mimic**: 10 chars. It puts the visual identity (neon) and the mechanic (mimicry) together in two common words. Turkish 'mimik' (facial expression) is neutral, and 'mimic/mimique/mimica' are clear in the European languages. Friendly and fine for 13+.
- **Twinshade**: 9 chars. It nods to the stage where a second shadow joins, and 'shade' sounds more poetic than 'shadow'. Easy to say. The only near-match is German 'schade' (what a pity), which sounds different and is mild, not offensive. Clean in the other languages.
- **Glowstep**: 8 chars, rhythmic and upbeat, suits a one-finger reflex game and the minimal geometric neon style. No negative readings in EN/TR/DE/IT/FR/ES. Works as an app icon wordmark.
- **Doppelglow**: 10 chars. 'Doppelganger' (German 'Doppel' = double) is known worldwide, so the meaning comes across right away while the coined word stays unique and playful. Pronounced DOP-el-glow. No negative meaning in any target language.
- **Umbrix**: 6 chars, short and distinctive, with a geometric, neon-arcade feel. Italian 'ombra' and Spanish 'umbra' make the shadow root recognisable. It is not a word in any target language, so it carries no negative meaning and is good for trademarking and app-store search, pending a name check.
- **Neon Rewind**: 11 chars. It shows the time-delay idea (your past replayed) and the neon look in two simple international words. It sounds retro-arcade, suits the 500-level / 100-map campaign, and has no negative connotations in EN/TR/DE/IT/FR/ES. Store title example: 'Neon Rewind: Shadow Escape' (26 chars).
- **Echoveer**: 8 letters and easy to say in every target language (eh-ko-VEER). Dutch 'veer' means feather/spring and is positive, and none of en/tr/de/it/fr/es gives it a negative reading. The V and the double E make a sharp, geometric neon wordmark, and the V can be drawn as the diamond character. Web check, Nov 2026: no game or app uses the exact word. Echo-prefixed browser titles do exist, though: 'Echo Dash' (neon arcade) and 'Echo Loop Runner' (your delayed path becomes a damage field). So it must always be written as one word to stay distinctive and protectable. I picked it over 'Echoflick' because in a glowing logo the 'fli' in that name can read as a profanity. Store title: 'Echoveer: Neon Shadow Dodge' (27 chars).
- **Ghostlap**: Of all the candidates, this one matches the replay-shadow mechanic most closely, and players get it at once from racing games. It is still a new compound, so it suits a trademark. Short (8), two strong syllables, and the o in GHOST can become the glowing hexagon. No negative meanings in en/tr/de/it/fr/es. I chose it over 'lag' blends because 'lag' means latency, a negative in games, and Turkish 'lağım' means sewer. Web check: no game or app named Ghostlap. Store title: 'Ghostlap: Beat Your Shadow' (26).
- **Umbrisk**: A coined single word that suits a trademark, with a sporty, energetic sound (um-BRISK) that fits a reflex arcade game. Umbra is understood through the Romance-language shadow words (it. ombra, es. sombra, fr. ombre) without copying any of them. Its only existing use is as the Scandinavian word for 'Umbrian' (the old Italian language/region), which is harmless. No negatives in en/tr/de/it/fr/es. I avoided 'shade' blends because German 'schade' means 'what a pity' and English 'throw shade' means an insult. Store title: 'Umbrisk: Shadow Reflex' (22).
- **Ombrix**: A fully coined, very distinctive 6-letter mark, which is ideal for search and trademark. It sounds punchy and arcade-like in every target language (Turkish readers say it 'ombriks' naturally). The O can be the round glowing player, with an X-shaped laser cross as the ending. Web check: no exact match (a game character 'Professor Umbrix' exists, spelled differently and in another category). No negatives in en/tr/de/it/fr/es. Store title: 'Ombrix: Outrun Your Shadow' (26).
- **Pastpulse**: It describes the replay-delay mechanic exactly, and the P-P alliteration makes it easy to remember. A pulse is also a natural neon/glow animation for the logo. I deliberately used 'Past' rather than 'After-' blends (Afterself, Afterwisp, Afterglide), because German 'After' means anus, which breaks the no-negative-meaning rule for DE. No negatives in en/tr/de/it/fr/es. Web check: no game or app named Pastpulse. Store title: 'Pastpulse: Shadow Arcade' (24).
- **Glintail**: Short (8), gentle and friendly-sounding, which suits a 13+ casual audience. The name holds the whole game in two images: a glowing hero and a pursuer on its tail. The shared T in 'glint/tail' gives a neat visual blend, and the dot of the i can be a gold star pickup. No negatives in en/tr/de/it/fr/es. Web check: no game or app named Glintail. Store title: 'Glintail: Shadow Escape' (23).
- **Trailtwin**: Plain English, but the compound is new and specific. The double T allows a mirrored logo (the second T as the dimmed teal shadow of the first, purple one), and 'twin' leads naturally into the 2-shadow stage. No negatives in en/tr/de/it/fr/es. Web check: no game called Trailtwin (only the unrelated 'Twin Trials' FPS on Steam, which is different enough). Store title: 'Trailtwin: Shadow Chase' (23).
- **Umbravoid**: A double pun: avoid your umbra, and the void (black hole) setting. Strong sci-fi neon tone that suits the late-game maps. Many games already use 'Umbra' on its own (UMBRA, Umbrafall, Umbra Survivors), so it must be used as one fused word, never 'Umbra Void', to stay distinctive. Web check: no exact match. No negatives in en/tr/de/it/fr/es ('void' here means space/emptiness). Store title: 'Umbravoid: Dodge Your Shadow' (28).
- **Phantrail**: Smoothly fused ('phantom' and 'trail' share the T sound). It feels mysterious and fast without being horror, which suits 13+. The 'ph' spelling makes it visually distinctive and easy to own in search. No negatives in en/tr/de/it/fr/es (es. fantasma, it. fantasma, fr. fantôme are all neutral). Web check: no game or app named Phantrail. Store title: 'Phantrail: Neon Shadow Run' (26).
- **Tracewisp**: A soft, magical feel that suits the 'Magic Lumos' brand and the glowing geometric characters. The wordmark could draw the 'wisp' as a fading trail after the solid 'Trace'. No negatives in en/tr/de/it/fr/es. Web check: no game or app named Tracewisp. Store title: 'Tracewisp: Shadow Arcade' (24).
